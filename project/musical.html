About the Project

A story that refused to stay in one medium.

This project grew out of family history and gradually became something stranger and more ambitious: part musical, part illustrated film, part visual memory piece.

It combines original songs, spoken scenes, handmade digital illustration, symbolic color, sparse movement, and a visual world that becomes increasingly unstable as buried truths begin to surface.

I’m documenting the process here as it develops — including sketches, finished scenes, revisions, musical decisions, visual experiments, production discoveries, and occasional creative dead ends.

August 2026

Act 2

Building a bigger world

Act 2 is opening the story outward. I’m adding town scenes, more environmental detail, and loose outline-only background figures so the world feels more populated without losing the handmade visual language established in Act 1.

The goal is to make the environment feel broader and more alive, while keeping the principal characters visually distinct through their saturated color coding.

Summer 2026

Milestone

Finishing Act 1

After several intense weeks of writing, recording, illustrating, and editing, Act 1 became a complete audiovisual piece running a little over half an hour.

Completing it also clarified the visual rules of the project: muted watercolor environments, highly saturated principal characters, repeated compositions, symbolic color, and selective moments of distortion as the story becomes more unstable.

Early Development

Visual language

Giving each character a color

One of the earliest visual choices was assigning each major character a specific saturated color while keeping most of the surrounding world in muted greys.

What began as an aesthetic choice quickly became part of the storytelling itself, letting relationships, memory, absence, and emotional shifts register visually without relying on conventional animation.
